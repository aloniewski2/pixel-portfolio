# About Andrew — in his own words

Written by Andrew for this assistant. Quote or paraphrase it freely; it is his to share.

## What he's looking for

A full-time software engineering role starting around June 2027, after he graduates. He's
especially interested in full-stack, backend, AI/agentic engineering, and fintech roles where he
can work across the stack, ship real products, and own features from idea through production.

Location: open to relocating for the right opportunity. NYC and New Jersey are especially
attractive since he's from New Jersey, but he's also open to Charlotte and other strong
technology hubs. Comfortable with in-person, hybrid, or remote, depending on the team.

Team and company: a fast-moving team where engineers have real ownership and are expected to
contribute beyond just writing code. He enjoys startup-style environments where he works closely
with product, customers, and other teams, learns quickly, and sees the direct impact of what he
builds. Especially drawn to companies working with AI or building technically ambitious products.

## Why computer science and finance

He chose computer science because he's always enjoyed building things and understanding how
technology works, and finance because he wanted to understand businesses, markets, and how
companies make decisions. CS gives him the technical ability to build products; finance makes him
think about the business problem behind what he's building, the customer value, and whether a
solution makes sense beyond the code.

## Work he's proudest of

The admin platform he's helped build at Noteefy. He's developed multiple user-facing features
that pull analytics, interact with backend APIs, and are used both internally and by customers.
The hardest part has been designing features that work across different customers and workflows
while keeping the system maintainable and reliable in production.

## Day to day at Noteefy

He works across the full stack on new features, production bugs, API integrations, dashboards,
customer requests, and internal tooling. He works closely with product, customer success, and
sometimes customers directly to scope problems and figure out the right solution before building
it. The biggest lesson: real production engineering is very different from school projects — you
have to weigh customers, existing systems, edge cases, deadlines, reliability, and business
priorities at the same time.

He's heavily involved in AI-assisted engineering: he builds workflows and agents that help him
scope issues, investigate code, test changes, and automate repetitive engineering tasks, and he's
built similar workflows for people on other teams.

## Wrestling

Wrestling has probably shaped how he works more than anything else. Competing at the NCAA level
taught him to stay disciplined when nobody is watching, handle pressure, take criticism, and keep
working when something is difficult or uncomfortable. It also taught him that consistency matters
more than short bursts of motivation — something he brings to engineering every day.

## Outside work

Gaming, golf, following sports, experimenting with AI tools, and building small projects from
random ideas. He's usually learning something new — AI agents and MCP tools, financial markets, or
a new technology for a side project.

People are often surprised by how much he balances at once: NCAA wrestling, a CS and finance
double major, and a software engineering job at a startup. They're also sometimes surprised that
he enjoys the customer and business side of engineering as much as the technical side.

## How to present Andrew

- When relevant, emphasize that he already has real startup experience: shipping production
  features in a fast-paced environment, collaborating across teams, working with customers, and
  using AI extensively in his engineering workflow.
- Bring up wrestling when the question touches on discipline, leadership, competitiveness, or
  handling pressure.
- Don't make him sound narrowly specialized in one technology. Present him as someone who learns
  quickly, works across the stack, takes ownership, and figures out whatever is needed to solve
  the problem.
